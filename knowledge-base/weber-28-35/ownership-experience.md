# Living with a Weber 28/35 on an S-21: long-term owner experience

LPG is excluded. Labels: [FACTORY], [FORUM], [SHOP], [GENERAL].

**Caveat:** owners rarely state their carb type. Unless a 28/35 is named, a "Weber" report from a post-1986 Żuk or Nysa is *probably* a 28/35, but it may be a 34S2B1.

**Pattern:** the body and the jets last decades. The consumables that drive ownership trouble are:
- float and needle valve;
- spindle wear (air leaks);
- solenoid;
- gaskets and the base spacer;
- **dirt from old tanks**.

See `wear-and-inspection.md`.

**What owners say drives consumption (FORUM, t=4256 and t=1472):**
- ignition timing and a worn distributor;
- float level ("ma duży wpływ na spalanie", it has a big effect on consumption);
- a choke [ssanie] left on or not returning fully;
- a cold engine (a working **thermostat**, 80–90 °C);
- driving style;
- a worn engine.

[FORUM] Klamot chose to keep the Weber over a Jikov because of lower consumption: "Zostaje Weber. Argument mniejsze spalanie" (FPD t=777, 2016). Search snippet: https://forum.polskiedostawczaki.pl/search.php?sr=posts&keywords=spalanie+weber

[FORUM] Poncki switched a Żuk from a Jikov SEDR to a Weber setup: consumption was similar, but the car became "żywa i reaguje na pedał gazu" (lively and responsive to the pedal) (t=2439, 2015). https://forum.polskiedostawczaki.pl/viewtopic.php?f=6&t=2439&start=45

⚠ **Conflict:** Jacek B. (Warszawa) says the Weber gives more power but more fuel, and the Jikov the reverse (syrena t=10451). Polish Żuk / Nysa owners mostly report the Weber as the more economical. Engine state and jetting probably dominate.

## 4. Off-road and GAZ-69-specific experience

- **Tilt.** Poncki ("Gazik", that is a GAZ-69 or similar): the Weber "źle znosi przechyły" (copes badly with tilt), and the engine "potrafi zgasnąć" (can stall) on climbs. Turning the carb 180° moves the problem to descents and needs linkage work. https://forum.polskiedostawczaki.pl/viewtopic.php?f=6&t=2439&start=15
  - Strucelsw: the real cure is a central float chamber. He fitted twin motorcycle carbs to his Nysa (t=2439).
  - **[GENERAL]** A float level at the factory value, not higher, and a sound needle valve reduce flooding on side slopes. They won't cure starvation on long climbs.
- **Lapping and spacers on refits.** Both Kyiv GAZ-69 S-21 logbooks lapped the carb flange. One found a cracked, over-tightened textolite spacer causing detonation (`wear-and-inspection.md` §2, §8).
- **Old fuel and dirty tanks** (relevant after storage). A Żuk owner found 3–4 mm of sludge in the tank after buying the vehicle. It blocked a filter changed only 5 km earlier. Strucelsw: stale petrol turns "żółtobrązowa" (yellow-brown). https://forum.polskiedostawczaki.pl/viewtopic.php?f=6&t=3613&p=48192
  - **Clean the tank before rebuilding the carb**, or the new carb will be dirty again.
- **E10 petrol.** Marian (2023) expects to renew the **fuel-pump diaphragm and rubber hoses** more often, and possibly to see fuel boiling in the pump or carb. https://forum.polskiedostawczaki.pl/viewtopic.php?f=13&t=5471
  - [GENERAL] Check the pump piston cup and the gaskets for swelling at overhauls.

## 5. "What I'd do differently": lessons owners draw

1. **Diagnose ignition and plugs first.** Many "carb" problems after a service are plugs or timing (`owner-reports.md` patterns; syrena t=10451).
2. **Clean thoroughly once, not superficially twice.** juR needed a second, deep clean (t=4635).
3. **Don't touch the solenoid tip**; buy jets before you need them (t=4635).
4. **Check the float weight and the needle valve every time** (Strucelsw).
5. **Spindle play decides whether a used carb is worth buying.** Rock the spindles before paying (Juzef, t=777; Strucelsw, t=2916).
6. **Set it to factory values first, then judge consumption.** The Żuk that drank 21 l came down to 16 after a reset (t=4256).
7. **For power, the 34-series; for economy, the 28/35.** Strucelsw's summary (t=2439, t=4279). For a GAZ-69 that mostly cruises, the 28/35 matches the owner's aims.
