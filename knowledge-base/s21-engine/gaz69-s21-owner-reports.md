# GAZ-69 with the S-21: owner reports

This file adds to `gaz69-swap.md` and does not repeat it. That file already covers: the S-21 bolts to the GAZ bellhousing and gearbox; the rear of the head can hit the firewall; mount holes may need re-drilling; both cars are 12 V; GAZ/ZMZ distributors fit; a Scénic filter, the DAAZ/Jikov carb options and the 2100 rpm habit.

## Read this first: what the evidence is

- **Polish swap threads are scarce.** Searches in Polish ("gazik S21", "GAZ 69 silnik żuka", "przekładka" and similar) found no Polish forum thread about S-21 problems in a GAZ-69. The Polish-language finds are one owner review, one magazine article and one UK register entry.
- **Most detail comes from two Russian-language drive2.ru logbooks** that record a full swap and two to four years of use:
  - **Car A**: GAZ-69, 1959, owner "Duplet59", Vitebsk region, Belarus. S-21 bought bare in 2018 (it came from a Nysa and had last been in a Pobeda). Single-choke Jikov carb, later a Chinese carb on a spare engine. Logbook: https://www.drive2.ru/r/gaz/491427822619853366/
  - **Car B**: GAZ-69, 1965, Kyiv region, Ukraine. S-21 fitted in 2020, **Weber carb**. Logbook: https://www.drive2.ru/r/gaz/69/549148609665302879/
- Labels used below:
  - `[FORUM]` means an owner's post or comment, not documentation.
  - **Confirmed** means the owner says the fix worked.
  - **Suggested** means advice, or a fix whose result was not reported.
- Quotes are short, in the original language. Every post is archived as text; see `sources-gaz69-s21.md`.

### Engine mounts, clutch, flywheel, gearbox and gearing

- **Mounts** (Car A). The old rubber mounts were soaked and perished by oil from the leaking M-20, so he replaced them before fitting the S-21. **Check mounts at any swap.** Confirmed. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/501164822717531371/
- **Clutch** (Car A). He fitted a new clutch, flywheel and driven disc to the S-21 before installation, and also overhauled the gearbox and its shift lever. Quote: "Сцепление, маховик и ведомый диск новые поставлю". [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/497671124520272357/
- **Clutch on Car A's spare engine.** It has the original diaphragm ("лепестковое") cover, a Russian disc and a new release bearing. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/625466811260865120/
- **Clutch fork** (Car B). He made a bracket for the clutch-fork return spring, because the release bearing is the constant-contact type. Confirmed fitted. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/596013918409722722/
- **Flywheel** (Car B, rebuild). Measured at 13.5 kg with 0.05 mm runout. Reference value only. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/605038160094711792/
- **Gearing** (Car B). With the original 41-tooth final drive, the S-21 pulls "like the M-20" from low revs and climbs hills noticeably better. On a 4-speed box he set off in 2nd and skipped to 4th. He planned 38-tooth final drives for a 60 to 70 km/h cruise. Suggested; the result was not reported. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/575155907952902834/
  - Later he went back to the original GAZ 3-speed box. He also found the front input-shaft pilot bearing sticking, and suspects it caused the synchro box to growl on shifts. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/596013918409722722/
- **Gearbox oil** (Car A). GL-5 oil chewed the bronze thrust washers in the GAZ gearbox. Commenters advise GL-4 or nigrol for Soviet boxes with bronze parts. This is not specific to the swap, but it is relevant to an overhaul. Suggested. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/605877362344598874/
- **Different engine, same gearbox (suggested).** Another owner fitted a ZMZ-417 (not an S-21) and kept the GAZ box. He found the GAZ 1st gear "fast", so the car was hard to start loaded uphill, and moved to a UAZ 4-speed box. [FORUM] comment on https://www.drive2.ru/l/502079066636026650/

### Starter, earthing and wiring

- **Key start** (Car A). The S-21 went in with an alternator and a key-operated (solenoid) starter instead of the GAZ foot starter, so he rewired from the original diagram. Quote: "гена переменник, а стартер от ключа". Confirmed working.
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/499238203467760267/
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/500541674502488516/
- **Earth strap** (Car A). A commenter advised fitting the engine-to-body earth strap **near the starter**, to keep high starting current short. The owner has a second strap; the original strap runs from the body to the sump. Suggested. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/502079066636026650/
- **Starter stays engaged** (Car B, original foot-pedal starter). The starter did not disengage after the pedal was released. Cause: the starter switch plunger was sticking, so its spring could not pull the contact disc back. Fix: he replaced the switch. Confirmed. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/611216865687005947/
- **Intermittent start fault** (Car A). He suspected the solenoid. The real cause was a badly soldered wire at the **ammeter**; he re-soldered all wires to the starter and cleaned the solenoid contacts. Confirmed. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/567967300930503782/
  - Commenters report that an ammeter wire nearly burned a car, and that they switched to a voltmeter. This is a warning, not a measured fault.

### Throttle linkage and cable

- **M-20 rod linkage** (Car B). He moved the whole M-20 linkage across with "no nuances". Problem: the pedal then sat too high ("педаль газа торчит в небо"). Fix: shorten the rod from the bellcrank to the carb. Confirmed. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/573452764441477168/ (image `swap-m20-throttle-linkage-pedal-high-drive2.jpg`)
- **Cable conversion** (Car B, Weber). The rod linkage gave jerky starts in 1st gear off-road, so he converted to a cable. He kept the hand throttle and the original pedal, and made the change reversible. Confirmed. The details:
  - a VAZ-2108 (Solex) throttle sector bolted to the Weber throttle lever, with the lever lengthened by 5 mm;
  - the spacer bushing on the throttle spindle thinned so the sector fits;
  - the cable outer stop welded to the air-cleaner body;
  - the 2108 inner cable shortened;
  - a rod with adjustment holes welded to the GAZ "spoon" pedal, plus an extra return spring.

  Result: the pedal is soft, without play, and gives smooth control. Images `swap-throttle-cable-2108-sector-drive2.jpg` and `swap-throttle-cable-stop-bracket-drive2.jpg`.
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/575155907952902834/
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/577092697685230198/

### Air cleaner

- **Car B tried three cleaners.**
  1. An AZLK-2141 (Moskvich) cleaner with a **metal** air-horn "turtle", because the plastic one would not seal to the carb. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/573452764441477168/
  2. A Moskvich barrel. It **boomed at mid revs**, being strapped to the firewall on the old oil-filter mount without rubber, and it blocked access to the right side of the carb.
  3. An **original Nysa paper-element housing**. Its height fits, with about 10 mm to the bonnet, but the intake snout had to be shortened where it hit the bonnet. It has a summer/winter warm-air flap. Quote: "родной корпус воздушного фильтра от Нысы". Confirmed. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/576061905534190250/ (image `swap-nysa-paper-air-filter-drive2.jpg`)
- **Car A** (Scénic housing; already listed in `gaz69-swap.md`). New details:
  - the clamp from the old oil filter was lengthened by a third to hold it;
  - the intake can be turned toward the exhaust manifold for winter.

  [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/504723941856641856/ (image `swap-scenic-air-filter-drive2.jpg`)
- **Mounting nuts.** A commenter notes that the air cleaner is normally held with special long nuts, not screws. Suggested. [FORUM] comment on https://www.drive2.ru/l/575155907952902834/

### Crankcase breather and oil separator

- **Car B: oil separator.** He drained it into the sump through a tee at the factory coarse-oil-filter (ФТО) drain hole, shared with the oil-cooler drain, and mounted it on the oiler-bracket holes. Image `swap-pcv-separator-drain-tee-drive2.jpg`. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/573452764441477168/
- **Car B: emulsion found.** The separator later held an **oil and water emulsion**, which should not go back into the sump. He built a larger separator from the old ФТО housing, with a drain hose for periodic emptying. Confirmed built. Quote: "Там не масло было а какая-то эмульсия". [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/576213913016730620/
- **Car A: breathing and oil loss.** He fitted a Hyundai/Kia 26740-45500 separator. Result: less oil thrown out, but "need to drive and see".
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/544690090014672020/
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/544851168468140062/
- **Car B: oil into the carb.** Oil thrown into the carb at high revs was one of the reasons he planned a rebuild. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/605038160094711792/

### Exhaust manifold, downpipe and muffler

- **Car B: cracked manifold.** The original cast-iron exhaust manifold was cracked, so he refurbished a spare. This is a new detail; the rest of that post is already summarised in `gaz69-swap.md`. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/573142427284540104/
- **Car B: downpipe hits the firewall.** The downpipe touched the firewall when the engine shook hard, so he added a tie-down clamp. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/573928852976304186/
  - The clamp then **rang under load**. He flattened the downpipe where it passes between the frame and the body and removed the clamp. Confirmed.
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/592926489758968214/
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/594018579683222158/
- **Car A: exhaust build.** A flexible section, a Chevrolet Niva muffler and a length of pipe, welded, with a home-made bracket to the running board. A commenter warns that home-made hangers knock and recommends the cheap UAZ-469 muffler mounting kit. Suggested. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/502803919676637895/
- **Car B: manifold gasket.** A new manifold gasket raised the idle, which showed the old gasket had been leaking. Confirmed. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/594018579683222158/

### Cooling: radiator, pump, pulleys, hoses and gauges

- **Car A: radiator.** The radiator leaked at first start; it had frozen together with the old engine, so it needed soldering. Later, **antifreeze made it weep**, where water had not, and he looked for a new radiator. Status: reported.
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/503849555234652534/
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/544690090014672020/
- **Car A: upper hose.** A commenter said the upper radiator hose routing "looks strange". The owner plans silicone hoses or re-soldering the radiator inlet. This suggests the S-21 thermostat outlet does not line up neatly with the GAZ radiator inlet. Suggested (inferred from photo comments). [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/504723941856641856/
- **Car B: heater tap.** The S-21 heater tap has **two outlets**. He blanked one, connected the other to the GAZ heater, and fixed the metal heater tube to an exhaust-manifold nut. Image `swap-heater-tap-coolant-hoses-drive2.jpg`. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/573452764441477168/
- **Water pump: the M-20 pump does not fit the S-21** (Car B). Quote: "от м20 не подходит". The S-21 pump is unique. He rebuilt a spare with 6203-2RS bearings and added a grease nipple; it has a face seal and a shaft seal. Confirmed. Image `swap-water-pump-s21-worn-vs-spare-drive2.jpg`.
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/577407158010774371/
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/590869853259170000/
- **Car B: pump bearings failing (root cause).**
  - Symptom: the new pump became noisy within weeks; it got worse in the cold, when the belt stiffened.
  - Cause: a **bent crankshaft pulley**. The replacement pulley's bore was slightly small, so it did not seat on the starting-dog centring collar, and over-tightening bent its hub.
  - Fix: he fitted an **M-20 crank pulley**, about 20 mm smaller in diameter, carefully centred, and checked runout with the engine running. Confirmed.
  - Image `swap-crank-pulley-s21-vs-m20-drive2.jpg`. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/581125706335912377/
- **Car B: greasing the pump.** The fan-hub collar blocks the grease gun on the pump nipple, so he machined a recess in the hub. Confirmed. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/584099610411147866/
- **Car A: false overheat lamp.** The overheat lamp lit as soon as the engine warmed. New senders did not help, and the lamp went out with the original MM7 sender fitted. The **real cause was the lamp wire shorted to earth** behind the dash. Confirmed. Quote: "коротил на массу провод лампочки". [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/540350042741933551/
- **Car B: dead temperature gauge.** The gauge dropped to zero, and a new sender and gauge did not fix it. He fitted a **capillary (mechanical) tractor thermometer**, 60 mm like the UAZ gauges, with a threaded fitting into the head, routed through the steering-column hole. Confirmed. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/590869853259170000/
- **Temperatures.**
  - Car B ran at 50 °C after 30 minutes idling at +9 °C. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/573928852976304186/
  - Car A "hardly heats up" after its decoking. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/562281726303273767/
  - **No owner reported real overheating in the GAZ engine bay.**
- **Winter** (Car A). A 220 V, 2 kW coolant pre-heater in the heater circuit takes 15 to 25 minutes to reach 40 °C at −6 to −10 °C. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/548646957485131028/

### Fuel system

- **Car A: electric pump.**
  - He fitted a Chinese **low-pressure electric pump** in the fuel line near the brake master cylinder. It hangs on its hoses, not frame-mounted, which the owner says reduces vibration.
  - The mechanical pump was removed and its opening blanked. There is **no return line**: the pump does not overpower the float valve.
  - Before starting, switch on the ignition and wait about 5 seconds for the bowl to fill.
  - Confirmed. Image `swap-electric-fuel-pump-location-drive2.jpg`.
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/544851168468140062/
  - [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/546417697659814820/
  - A later post says the mechanical pump was "left in place for now" as a backup. This contradicts the earlier "removed and blanked" post. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/548646957485131028/
- **Car A: tank.** The tank was full of rust. He flushed it, replaced the pickup strainer with a shortened UAZ one, and swapped the sediment bowl for an injection-engine filter. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/548646957485131028/
- **Car B: back to factory layout.** He returned to the original order: tank, sediment bowl, mechanical pump, serviceable fine filter, carb. His reason: everything can be cleaned in the field. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/582315103039499779/
- **Car B: oil at the fuel-pump flange.** The oil-pump relief valve stuck at first start. Pressure reached 6 atm and **pushed oil out past the fuel-pump gasket**. Fix: he replaced the worn oil-pump cover. Confirmed. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/573452764441477168/
  - At the rebuild he also lapped the fuel-pump flange and used a thin paronite (compressed-fibre) gasket. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/594018579683222158/
- **Car B: fuel cap.** A **Nysa locking fuel cap** fits the GAZ filler. He used a Volga GAZ-24 lock barrel and filed the slots to suit. Confirmed. [FORUM] https://www.drive2.ru/l/608920260774483191/
- **Vapour lock near the exhaust: no reports found.**

## 3. Military overhaul conversion or hobby swap?

- **Documented in a register.** The UK GAZ-69 register lists a 1969 GAZ-69 imported from Poland in 2017 with an OHV "Zuk" engine "retrofitted by the Polish Military". This is the owner's statement in the register, not works paperwork. [REGISTER] https://gaz69.org/gaz/register.html
- **Not every Polish Army GAZ-69 got one.** The same register lists a 1971 GAZ-69 decommissioned by a Warsaw army unit in 2010 that still has the 2.1 L side-valve engine and was "never rebuilt". [REGISTER] same URL

## Swap checklist (S-21 GAZ-69 that runs badly)

1. **Ignition first.** Check the condenser: swap in a known-good old one, since new condensers were the culprit in Car A. Then the points, rotor and cap tracking. On Lada contactless conversions, check the distributor clamp and the timing index, because the oil-pump gear was re-indexed.
2. **Carb base.** Check the textolite spacer for cracks and warping; a crack gives a lean mixture and pinking at 40 km/h. Check the carb flange is flat and the manifold gasket is not leaking (a high idle is a clue). Make sure the throttle returns fully.
3. **Throttle.** On an M-20 linkage, check that the rod length lets the pedal reach full throttle. On a cable, check the return springs.
4. **Fuel.**
   - Rust in the tank and the pickup strainer.
   - Sediment bowl and filter.
   - Electric pump: wait about 5 s before cranking.
   - Fuel-pump flange leaks, including oil weeping there from high oil pressure.
5. **Oil.**
   - Cold pressure should be about 4 atm and hot idle about 1 to 2 atm (owner figures).
   - If it is low: check the oil filter brand, then a worn pump. An M-20 pump fits.
   - If it is very high: check for a stuck relief valve.
6. **Breather and separator.** Drain any emulsion. Check the separator hose is on the correct carb fitting, and look for oil carried over into the carb.
7. **Cooling.**
   - Check crank pulley runout: a bent pulley destroys the unique S-21 pump.
   - Check the pump bearings and grease them.
   - Check the upper-hose alignment and look for radiator weeping on antifreeze.
   - On a "hot" lamp or gauge, check the wire for a short to earth before you blame the sender.
8. **Valve train.** Look for bent or broken pushrods (missing on one cylinder), tappet noise and chipped timing-gear teeth (a "diesel" knock). Check the oil pickup is tight.
9. **Head.** If it burns oil, suspect worn guides and missing stem seals on old heads.
10. **Exhaust.** Check where the downpipe meets the firewall and the frame-to-body gap (ringing, knocking), the muffler hangers, and the manifold for cracks.
11. **Electrics.**
    - The ammeter wire and solder joints.
    - The earth strap near the starter.
    - The starter switch plunger (foot starter).
    - The alternator pulley wall and bearing.
    - On dead gauges, consider UAZ fuel gauge and sender pairs, or a capillary temperature gauge.
12. **Firewall and heater.** After firewall dressing, re-seal the heater box.

## Patterns

- **Throttle and air cleaner are the fiddly parts.** Owners repeatedly worked on the throttle drive (pedal height, then cable conversion) and the air cleaner (three designs in Car B). The original **Nysa paper-filter housing** fitted best.
- **Cooling did not mean overheating.** Cooling problems were about **parts compatibility**, not heat:
  - the M-20 pump does not fit;
  - the S-21 pump is unique and scarce;
  - a bent pulley kills pumps;
  - hose routing is awkward;
  - old radiators leak.

  Neither owner reported true overheating.
- **Classic old-engine faults dominate running problems**: condensers, a cracked carb spacer, stuck oil relief valves, worn valve guides and bent pushrods. These are not specific to the swap, but they appear early after fitting an engine that has stood for a long time.
- **Parts mix.** S-21 and M-20 parts mix freely in practice: oil pump, crank pulley and crankshaft. The water pump is the exception.
- **Gearing.** The 3-speed GAZ box with stock axles limits cruising to about 50 to 60 km/h. Owners consider axle-ratio or gearbox changes instead of revving the S-21, and both keep it under about 2000 rpm.
- **Electrics are rewired anyway.** The swap is usually combined with an alternator, key start, voltmeter, contactless ignition and an electric pump, so electrical faults (shorts, a bad ammeter wire) follow.
